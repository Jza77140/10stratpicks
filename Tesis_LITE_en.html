1 Business model

Lumentum builds the light that moves data inside an AI data center. It closed fiscal 2026 on June 27, 2026 with revenue of $3,014.0M, up 83.2% from $1,645.0M the prior year, and the final quarter alone billed $1,006.3M (+109.3% year over year and +24.5% sequentially). It makes money in two places with very different economics. Components — $649.4M in the quarter, 64.5% of the total, +102.7% year over year — are chips: indium phosphide (InP) lasers, the EMLs that sit inside every 800G and 1.6T transceiver, ultra-high-power lasers for co-packaged optics (CPO), and telecom line components. That is where the margin lives and where the industry bottleneck sits. Systems — $356.9M, 35.5%, +122.6% — is assembled product: the cloud modules that came with the Cloud Light acquisition, optical circuit switches (OCS) and telecom line systems. It carries a far higher ASP per unit but a lower margin than the bare chip, something management itself acknowledged when it announced its first ELS module order. Operating leverage during the year was brutal: GAAP gross margin went from 28.0% to 41.7% and GAAP operating margin from −10.9% to +17.4% ($524.8M of operating income); on a non-GAAP basis operating margin jumped from 9.7% to 29.8% and adjusted EBITDA from $264.2M to $1,025.8M. Two warnings that the income statement does not make obvious. First: GAAP net income for the year was −$6,935.1M, but there is no business impairment behind it — it is a NON-cash $7,756.6M loss on debt extinguishment, created by retiring the 2026, 2028 and 2029 Convertible Notes with shares that were worth roughly eight times more than when the notes were issued. The clean read on the year is non-GAAP net income of $782.3M ($8.67 per diluted share). Second: trailing twelve-month free cash flow was only $300.1M against that $782.3M, because property and equipment grew from $726.4M to $1,159.1M, inventories from $470.1M to $691.6M and receivables from $250.0M to $520.3M. The fabs get paid for before anything ships.

Three new ramps stack on top of the transceiver business: optical circuit switches (OCS), with a stated $400M outlook for the second half of calendar 2026 and the first triple-digit-million quarter already expected in fiscal Q1; ultra-high-power CPO lasers, where management says the lead customer's demand signal has increased and high-volume shipments arrive in the second half of calendar 2027 for 2028 deployments; and ELS modules, with a first order for delivery in the second half of 2027. Physical capacity sits behind all of it: Lumentum is expanding its two InP wafer fabs in Japan to attack 200G and 300G per-lane speeds, and added a fifth InP fab — the former Qorvo plant in Greensboro, North Carolina — with first shipments planned for 2028. Funding that expansion is the corporate event of the year: on March 2, 2026 NVIDIA subscribed $2,000M of Series A convertible preferred stock (2,876,415 shares at $695.31 each, convertible one-for-one into common) inside a multi-year partnership that funds R&D and the manufacturing footprint in North Carolina and Japan. The CEO set the next bar at $2,000M of revenue per quarter — $8,000M annualized, close to triple all of fiscal 2026 — and said new financial targets will be presented at the next OFC. That announcement is the concrete test: if the targets he publishes land below the run-rate the market is already paying for, the problem will not be the business, it will be the price.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Two markets get confused all the time and need to be separated. In the chip, Lumentum owns the bottleneck: the industry estimates it holds 50% to 60% of the global EML laser market, with demand running roughly 30% above supply and capacity committed under long-term agreements through calendar 2027. The only comparable vertically integrated competitor is Coherent (COHR); Broadcom pushes from the silicon photonics and co-packaged optics flank and Marvell from the DSP side. In the module, by contrast, Lumentum is a small player: InnoLight has led global transceiver shipments for the third consecutive year at 23.4% in 2025 per LightCounting, Coherent runs around 25% share, and industry sources estimate InnoLight and Eoptolink together supply roughly 60% of NVIDIA's 800G volume. Fabrinet does not compete: it assembles for nearly everyone, so it gets paid whoever wins the module. The moat verdict is moderate, and it lives in the chip, not in the module. In favor: an InP wafer fab is not bought, it is learned — yield is built over years — and very few hands can do it at scale today. Against it, three concrete things. Modules are commoditizing and the move to 1.6T shifts the mix toward silicon photonics (the industry estimates roughly a 30/70 split between EML and SiPh in 2026, against an 800G generation dominated by EML), which is exactly Broadcom's and the Chinese module makers' ground. The EML shortage is a market condition, not a patent: it is cured with capacity, and everyone is building capacity. And the moat rests on two buyers: in fiscal 2026 one customer accounted for 26.6% of revenue and another for 15.0% — 41.6% between them — and one of them is also a shareholder. A moat that depends on two customers is a moat those two customers can price.
Owned indium phosphide fabs
Four InP wafer fabs in production plus a fifth bought from Qorvo in Greensboro for 2028. InP wafer yield is learned over years and with heavy capital: that is the real barrier in this business, not assembly.
Dominant share of the EML chip
The industry estimates 50%-60% of the global EML market, with demand running about 30% above supply and capacity committed under long-term agreements through calendar 2027.
NVIDIA as a strategic anchor
$2,000M of Series A preferred subscribed on March 2, 2026 at $695.31 per share inside a multi-year partnership funding R&D and the North Carolina and Japan fabs. It aligns the roadmap with whoever sets the standard.
Vertical integration from chip to module
With Cloud Light it can sell the laser and also the assembled 800G and 1.6T transceiver; module makers competing on price depend on buying the chip from someone.
Requalification cost inside the customer's platform
A laser already qualified inside a hyperscaler platform does not get swapped mid-generation: requalifying costs quarters. It is the softest of the five moats and gets renegotiated at every speed jump.

4 Risks● Key risk: High

The price already discounts the entire planAt $881.25 — against a 52-week range of $144.52 to $1,085.68 and a +490% year — and adding the 88.6M common shares, the 2.9M NVIDIA preferred and the ~14M that come out of converting the 2032 Notes, fully diluted market cap is around $95,000M, not the $77,945M of the simple calculation. Even delivering the $2,000M quarterly revenue target ($8,000M annualized) at a 40% non-GAAP operating margin and a 16.5% tax rate, earnings would be on the order of $25 per share: at 25x that is worth ~$625. Today's price demands the full plan AND an exit multiple near 35x, against a consensus five-year growth expectation of just 12.96%.
Concentration: two customers are 41.6% of revenueIn fiscal 2026 one customer was 26.6% of revenue and another 15.0%. One of them is simultaneously a preferred shareholder, which helps the roadmap but does not improve pricing power.
Hyperscaler capex cycleRevenue went from $1,645.0M to $3,014.0M in twelve months on the investment decisions of a handful of buyers. A slowdown does not show up as a smaller order, it shows up as a cancellation, and the build-up signals are already there: inventories rose from $470.1M to $691.6M and receivables from $250.0M to $520.3M.
Migration to silicon photonics and co-packaged opticsThe move to 1.6T shifts the mix from EML toward silicon photonics, and CPO redefines where the laser lives. If the shift accelerates before the new fabs produce, today's scarce asset stops being scarce and the capex will already be spent.
Cash does not follow the earningsTrailing twelve-month free cash flow was $300.1M against $782.3M of non-GAAP net income: property and equipment grew from $726.4M to $1,159.1M and working capital ate the rest. The fragile assumption is that demand is still there when the Greensboro and Japan fabs come online in 2028.

5 Management

LiderazgoMichael Hurlston, CEO since 2024 and previously at Synaptics, turned Lumentum from telecom toward data center optics. He committed to publishing new financial targets at the next OFC: that is the concrete number to measure him against.
CapitalHe swapped the 2026, 2028 and 2029 Convertible Notes for shares and subscribed $2,000M of preferred with NVIDIA. Result: long-term debt fell from $2,562.6M to $40.5M and the company ended with roughly $1,100M of net cash ($2,043.5M of cash plus $694.9M of investments against $1,637.4M of debt). The cost was real dilution — additional paid-in capital jumped from $1,986.8M to $12,430.1M — plus a $7,756.6M non-cash accounting loss. Zeroing the debt right before a capex cycle is defensible; the dilution does not reverse.
PlanFive InP fabs (four in production, Greensboro for 2028), OCS with a $400M outlook for the second half of calendar 2026, a first ELS module order for 2027 and a stated target of $2,000M of revenue per quarter. That entire plan is paid for with today's capex and only gets collected in 2027-2028.
