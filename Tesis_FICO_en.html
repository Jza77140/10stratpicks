1 Business model

Fair Isaac produces the FICO Score, the standard for U.S. consumer credit, licensed to bureaus and lenders on every pull. Alongside it runs the FICO Platform, its decision software for fraud, origination and collections, with recurring revenue. Scores are ~53% of sales and ~60% of profit; Software is the rest. EBIT margin 42% and FCF at 47% of sales, with negative equity after years of buybacks and net debt/EBITDA of 4.2x.

Growth drivers: the center of gravity has shifted to software — migrating customers to the cloud-based FICO Platform and expanding in fraud and banking outside the U.S. The lever that powered the last few years, raising the mortgage score royalty, has been in doubt since September 2026.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Until 4-Sep-2026 the advantage came from a de facto monopoly backed by mandate: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac accepted only FICO scores, which made any price increase enforceable. That day the FHFA cleared VantageScore for all lenders at both agencies, effective immediately. The standard survives outside mortgage — auto, cards, consumer lending, lenders' internal models and securitization documents are still written on FICO — but the leg that guaranteed pricing power is no longer guaranteed.
