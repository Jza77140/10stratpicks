1 Business model

Tennessee bank holding company and owner of SmartBank (founded in 2007, headquartered in Pigeon Forge), with 42 branches and two loan production offices across East and Middle Tennessee, Alabama, Florida and Georgia. Its subsidiary Fountain Equipment Finance funds heavy equipment, semi-trucks and trailers throughout the Southeast, with offices in Knoxville, Atlanta, Memphis, Nashville and Birmingham. At 31-Dec-2025 it held $5,861M in assets, $4,364M in loans and leases (74% of assets) and $5,153M in deposits. In 2025 net interest income was $166.1M and noninterest income $34.4M, for net income of $50.3M, or $2.98 per diluted share (+39%). The tax-equivalent NIM was 3.29%, with a 0.91% ROA, 9.67% ROE and a 65.45% efficiency ratio. It reports a single segment, the General Banking Unit.

Growth drivers: organic growth, with $453.8M of additional loans and leases and $466.3M of additional deposits during the year, both at record highs; disciplined acquisitions, which the strategic plan explicitly names as the second engine alongside organic growth; equipment finance through Fountain Equipment Finance across the Southeast, a higher-yielding product than traditional banking; and balance sheet repositioning, with $100M of subordinated debt issued, $40M of prior debt retired, the sale of SBK Insurance at a $4.0M pre-tax gain and the restructuring of $85M of the available-for-sale portfolio.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

It competes in growing Southeastern markets — Tennessee, Alabama, Florida and Georgia — but does so against far larger regional banks and without a product advantage. The numbers confirm it: a 3.29% NIM and a 65.45% efficiency ratio are middle-of-the-road figures, far from elite community banking. Its real edge is geographic rather than structural: being established in markets that grow. Scale is still not enough to dilute operating costs.
