1 Business model

Cerebras sells artificial intelligence compute built on the Wafer Scale Engine, a chip that occupies an entire 300-millimetre wafer — roughly 46,000 mm² against about 800 for a GPU — and therefore avoids splitting the model across thousands of chips. The current generation is the WSE-3; in August 2026 it launched the CS-4 "Nexus" system, which does not carry a WSE-4 but three overclocked WSE-3 units per rack, 750 PFLOPs. The sales argument is inference latency, not operations per dollar. It makes money two ways and the mix has already flipped: in the first half of 2026 systems sales contributed $164.7 million and were down 23% year on year in the second quarter, while the inference cloud contributed $208.8 million, growing 282%. The problem is that the cloud carries the thin margin: consolidated gross margin fell from 31.1% in Q2 2025 to 14.2% in Q2 2026. LTM through 30-Jun-2026 shows revenue of $680.7 million, an operating loss of -$552.5 million and free cash flow of -$680.2 million; against that sits $7,003 million of net cash after the IPO. No P/E is published because there is no profit: the only year with positive net income, FY2025 at +$237.8 million, was produced by a $363.3 million non-operating gain on extinguishment of a forward contract liability; the non-GAAP result that year was -$75.7 million.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

On the other side stand NVIDIA with the entire CUDA ecosystem, AMD with the MI line, Google's TPUs in-house, and on the same latency axis the direct competitors Groq and SambaNova. The moat is weak and it is worth being explicit about why: first, Cerebras software does not create a switching cost comparable to CUDA, which is the sector's real barrier; second, latency performance is replicable — Groq competes exactly there — and is protected by neither contract nor network effect; third, fabricating an entire defect-free wafer depends on TSMC with no second source. What Cerebras has is an engineering and time-to-market advantage, not a defensible position. On top of that, demand is concentrated in an unusual way: in FY2025 86% of revenue came from two Emirati entities disclosed as related parties, MBZUAI at about 62% and G42 at about 24%; in FY2024 G42 alone had been 85%. In Q2 2026 three customers accounted for 76% of the quarter, and as of 31-Dec-2025 a single customer represented 78% of accounts receivable.
Wafer-scale architecture
The WSE-3 integrates on a single die what a GPU needs thousands of interconnected chips to do, eliminating node-to-node communication latency. It is a genuine design advantage, but an engineering one, not a contractual one.
Leadership in inference latency
The positioning is not performance per dollar but response speed, an axis it currently leads. Groq competes on the same axis, so the lead must be revalidated with each generation.
Contracted backlog and the OpenAI relationship
About $25,400 million declared, including a master agreement of roughly $20,000 million for 750 MW. It provides visibility, but it also turns a single counterparty into customer, creditor and shareholder simultaneously.
Installed capacity as an entry barrier
$548.9 million of capex in one half-year plus $1,500 million of committed leases: anyone wanting to compete in inference at this scale has to put up the same capital first.
Founding team with a proven track record
Andrew Feldman and his team already built and sold SeaMicro to AMD for $334 million in 2012 and brought the know-how to Cerebras in 2016. That is human capital, not a structural barrier.

4 Risks● Key risk: High

Sovereign concentration of demand86% of FY2025 revenue from two related parties in the United Arab Emirates, and 78% of receivables in a single customer at the end of 2025. A change in export policy or a political shift in the UAE is not a multiple discount: it is an existential risk.
Gross margin collapsedFrom 31.1% to 14.2% in one year through the mix shift toward the cloud. Scaling the growing business worsens the margin before improving it, and there is no evidence yet of the level at which it stabilises.
Capex and off-balance-sheet leases$548.9 million of capex in one half-year, $1,500 million of committed leases not yet commenced as of 30-Jun-2026 and another $753.4 million as of 30-Sep-2026. The $7,003 million of net cash is consumed quickly at this pace.
OpenAI is creditor, customer and shareholder at onceThe $1,000 million loan is settled through services and the warrants over 43.5 million shares at $0.00001 equal about 18% of current shares. Any renegotiation hits all three lines at the same time.
No audited track record as a public companyIt has not yet filed a 10-K. It is an emerging growth company and a non-accelerated filer, so there is no auditor opinion on internal control under Sarbanes-Oxley section 404(b), and the LTM is a hybrid of the S-1 and the two existing 10-Qs.

5 Management

LiderazgoAndrew Feldman, co-founder and CEO since 2016, had already founded and sold SeaMicro to AMD for $334 million in 2012 and brought part of that team along. Triple-class A/B/N structure: Class B concentrates the votes of founders and insiders, so control does not sit with the float.
CapitalIt went public on 13-May-2026 at $185 per share, above the range, raising $5,550 million (about $6,230 million net in the cash flow statement). It had filed an S-1 in 2024 seeking roughly $8,000 million and withdrew it over the CFIUS review of the G42 investment. It now holds $7,003 million of net cash with the OpenAI loan as its only debt.
PlanPivot from systems sales to cloud inference services, install the 750 MW committed with OpenAI, and diversify the customer base to reduce dependence on the UAE. The fragile assumption is that gross margin recovers as it scales; so far it is doing the opposite.
