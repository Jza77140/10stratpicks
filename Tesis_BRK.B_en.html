1 Business model

A conglomerate built on its insurers' float. Berkshire is an Omaha-based holding company that owns outright a collection of operating businesses and, through its insurers, a portfolio of listed stocks and a mountain of Treasury bills. Four pillars: (1) insurance —GEICO (third-largest U.S. auto insurer, ~11.6% share), the Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group and the Reinsurance Group— which generate the float: policyholder funds the company invests for its own account, $176 billion at year-end 2025 versus $138 billion in 2020; (2) BNSF, one of the two major railroads of the western U.S., with ~35,000 employees; (3) Berkshire Hathaway Energy, regulated utilities, pipelines and renewables; and (4) a hundred manufacturing, service and retail businesses (Precision Castparts, Lubrizol, Marmon, Clayton Homes, McLane, See's…). Each Class B share equals 1/1,500 of a Class A; the FY2025 10-K was filed on Feb 28, 2026 and since Jan 1, 2026 the CEO is Greg Abel, with Warren Buffett as Chairman.

The 2025 numbers (10-K): consolidated revenues of $371,444 M (premiums earned $88,902 M, sales and services $199,524 M, railroad-utilities-energy $49,723 M, interest and dividends $23,261 M). Net earnings attributable were $66,968 M ($31.04 per B share) versus $88,995 M in 2024, but that figure mixes apples and oranges: it includes $30,737 M of investment gains (mark-to-market of the portfolio) and −$8,255 M of impairments on Kraft Heinz and Occidental. What matters is after-tax operating earnings, ~$44.5 billion: insurance underwriting $7,258 M, insurance investment income $12,513 M, BNSF $5,476 M, BHE $3,979 M, manufacturing-service-retail $13,647 M and other $1,613 M. Balance sheet: $369 billion in cash and Treasury bills ($47,719 M + $321,434 M), $297,778 M in listed equities, shareholders' equity of $717,419 M (+$68.1 billion in the year) and total assets of $1.22 trillion. No buybacks in Q4 2025 and no dividend since 1967.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

It does not compete in one market: it competes in all of them, with a balance sheet nobody else has. In auto insurance GEICO fights State Farm, Progressive, Allstate and USAA (the top five hold ~63.6% of the market); in reinsurance Berkshire is the only player able to write catastrophe policies worth tens of billions without a partner, thanks to statutory surplus of $333 billion and AA+ (S&P) / A++ (A.M. Best) ratings. BNSF shares the West with Union Pacific in a duopoly regulated by the STB; BHE operates under rates set by state commissions. The equity portfolio remains concentrated: the five largest (American Express, Apple, Bank of America, Coca-Cola and Chevron) are 65% of the total, down from 71% in 2024. The strategic asset is optionality: with $369 billion of liquidity, Berkshire is the natural buyer of any large asset sold in a hurry during a crisis, as it did in 2008-2009 and 2020.
