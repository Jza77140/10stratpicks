1 Business model

China's leading online recruitment platform. Its BOSS Zhipin app connects the hiring boss directly with the candidate over chat, bypassing the HR department and the classic résumé middleman. Almost all monetisation sits on the employer side: in Q2-2026 revenue from enterprise customers was RMB 2,384.0M (US$351.4M), 99.4% of total revenue of RMB 2,398.6M (US$353.5M), up 14.1% year on year; paid services to job seekers contributed just RMB 14.6M and are being deliberately wound down. The free side is what holds the business together: 70.2M average monthly active users in the quarter (+10.4%), an all-time high, and 7.2M paid enterprise customers in the twelve months to June 2026 (+10.8%).

What is in play: Q2-2026 showed real operating leverage — income from operations rose 32.6% to RMB 863.2M (US$127.2M) and the operating margin expanded 5.0 percentage points — despite a 38.3% jump in sales and marketing driven by the 2026 FIFA World Cup campaigns. Reported net income of RMB 1,942.3M (+173.1%) is inflated by investment results: adjusted net income of RMB 1,029.2M (US$151.7M), up 9.4%, is the number to watch. Q3-2026 guidance: revenue of RMB 2.41–2.50 billion (+11.4% to +15.6%). Cash, short-term deposits and short-term investments total RMB 18,807M (US$2,772M) against no meaningful financial debt. Shareholder returns are aggressive: an annual dividend of US$0.255 per ordinary share (US$0.510 per ADS, roughly US$230M) plus over US$300M of buybacks year to date, with the programme extended to US$400M through August 2027; together they already exceed 100% of the prior year's adjusted net income.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

The market watches three things. (1) The network effect against China's employment cycle: paid enterprise customers are the thermometer of whether the network keeps taking share while the labour market cools; growing customers 10.8% with MAUs at a record is the evidence that the moat still works even when the macro does not help. (2) Whether the operating leverage is structural or a spending pause: margins expanded 5 points while marketing rose 38%, which suggests the savings came from G&A (−29.7%) and efficiency rather than from cutting growth; the question is what remains once AI spending normalises. (3) What the cash pile and the China discount are worth: nearly US$2.8bn of liquidity against a roughly US$7.2bn market cap means the operating business trades at a far lower multiple than the headline; the market is discounting the VIE structure and regulatory risk, and the pace of buybacks and dividends is management's way of arguing with that discount. AI commercialisation (AI-hosted chats and interviews) is the growth option not yet in the numbers.
