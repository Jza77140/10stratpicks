1 Business model

SK hynix is the world's second-largest memory manufacturer and the largest in high-bandwidth memory (HBM), with 56.4% of the global market according to IDC in Q1 2026. In Q2 2026 DRAM contributed 73% of revenue and NAND 27%. The LTM through June 2026 shows revenue of $140,492 million, operating income of $95,586 million — a 68.0% operating margin — and net income of $120,396 million, with net cash of $51,542 million. Now the detail that changes the reading: almost all of the revenue jump is price, not volume. In Q2 2026 DRAM bit volume rose only high single digits while the average selling price rose about 30% in the quarter and 198% year on year; NAND ASP rose about 50%. Utilisation is already at 100%. And roughly $43,000 million of net income is not operating: the sale and revaluation of the Kioxia stake booked a KRW 62.17 trillion extraordinary pre-tax gain in June, which is why quarterly net income exceeds operating income. The 10.6x P/E on the ADR becomes about 17.5x if calculated on taxed EBIT, which is the honest base. One more instrument-specific point: the ratio is 10 ADSs per Seoul ordinary share, and the ADS trades at a 42.6% premium to the Korean line.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

DRAM is a three-way oligopoly: Samsung Electronics, SK hynix at 29.1% and Micron together hold more than 90% of the market. In NAND, SK hynix is second with 18.5% including Solidigm. The moat is moderate and its nature matters: it is neither purely technological nor contractual, it is capital and time. The industry is investing on the order of $237,000 million of capex in 2026 with 87.6% concentrated in twenty companies, and a new fab takes years to produce. Management itself defines its differentiator as production yield, quality and the ability to deliver volume on schedule, not chip superiority. That is a real moat while scarcity lasts and it evaporates when capacity normalises: exactly what happened in 2023, when the operating margin was -23.6%. There is also a nuance worth understanding properly because it is the heart of the current cycle: the engine is not HBM selling at a high price, it is that HBM ate the wafers that used to make conventional DRAM, which is why traditional DRAM prices rose 198% year on year. That is an artefact of capacity allocation, and it is the line most exposed to reversal when new capacity arrives.
HBM leadership
56.4% of the global market according to IDC in Q1 2026, first to produce HBM3 and HBM3E, and HBM4 already in mass production since Q2 2026 with a full ramp in the second half.
Oligopolistic DRAM structure
Three players hold more than 90% of the market. Capacity discipline among few actors is what sustains prices, and also what sinks them when one of them decides to grow.
Capital and lead-time barrier
$39,700 million of capex already contracted as of 30-Jun-2026 plus $44,600 million approved afterwards. An entrant needs that capital and several years before selling a single bit.
Long-term agreements with deposits
About ten customers on roughly five-year contracts with prepayments. The company does not disclose what share of sales they cover, so the value of this advantage cannot be verified from outside.
Qualification relationship with the anchor customer
Being the qualified HBM supplier to the leading artificial intelligence accelerator maker is a hard position to displace within a product generation, though it is revalidated at every one.

4 Risks● Key risk: High

The 2027 export licence for China is not securedValidated End-User status was revoked on 31-Dec-2025 and replaced by annual approval. The 2026 licence was obtained; the 2027 one is not. The Wuxi and Dalian fabs depend on it and they are not marginal assets.
Capex overhang against normalising prices$39,700 million contracted as of 30-Jun-2026 against $4,600 million six months earlier. Depreciation goes from about $9,800 million in 2025 to about $26,000 million by 2028, arriving just as prices should normalise: the exact sequence that produced the 2023 loss.
Quality of reported earningsAbout $43,000 million of LTM net income is the Kioxia gain, not operations. The 7.4x trailing P/E on the Seoul line rises to 12.3x on taxed EBIT, and the ADR's 10.6x to about 17.5x.
42.6% ADR premiumAn instrument risk, not a company risk: you pay 42.6% more than in Seoul for the same cash flow. The arbitrage is blocked because the conversion quota of 17,790,000 shares is already exhausted; if it is widened, that is a negative catalyst for the ADS and neutral for the underlying.
Extreme cyclicality and a currency headwindOperating margin went from -23.6% in 2023 to 76.3% in Q2 2026: 98 points in 30 months. The company also states that a 10% move in the dollar is worth about $1,900 million of annual pre-tax income, and the dollar has fallen more than 10% against the won since 02-Jul-2026, that is, after the 29-Jul guidance.
