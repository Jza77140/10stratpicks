1 Business model

Financial holding company of Old National Bank, tracing its roots to 1834, with corporate headquarters in Evansville, Indiana, and commercial and consumer banking run from Chicago. It is the sixth largest Midwestern-headquartered bank and ranks among the top 25 in the U.S.: it operates 346 banking centers across Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, North Carolina, North Dakota, Tennessee and Wisconsin, with 4,971 employees. It closed 2025 with $72.2 billion in assets, $48.8 billion in loans and $55.1 billion in deposits after integrating Bremer Bank. Net interest income grew 34% to $2,058 million with a 3.54% NIM and noninterest income added $467 million. Net income was $669 million —$653 million attributable to common shareholders—, with a 1.02% ROA, 15.27% ROTCE and a 55.10% efficiency ratio.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Mid-sized community and commercial banking built through mergers: it now ranks among the 25 largest U.S. bank holding companies and is the sixth largest headquartered in the Midwest by assets. It competes with much larger banks, credit unions and fintechs, and its defense is branch density in secondary markets where the local relationship still matters. The brand carries nearly two centuries of continuity.
