1 Business model

GE Vernova is the energy business General Electric spun off in April 2024, with $40,061M in LTM revenue across three segments. Power (heavy-duty and aeroderivative gas turbines, nuclear, hydro and steam) posted $10,449M of revenue in H1-2026, +13%, with segment EBITDA of $1,842M and an 17.6% margin: it sells the machine once and then collects long-term maintenance contracts on an installed base of more than 7,000 gas turbines, which is where the real margin sits. Electrification (transformers, switchgear, HVDC and grid integration) posted $6,597M, +65% —only +29% organic, the rest being the Prolec GE consolidation— with an 18.2% EBITDA margin and an equipment backlog of $40,600M, up 69% year over year. Wind posted $3,459M, −16%, and lost $657M of EBITDA in the half. The H1 mix was $11,713M of equipment against $8,729M of services, and that ratio flips in the backlog: of the $176,284M of committed orders at June 30, 2026, $88,463M are services and $87,821M equipment. Equipment is sold to plant the installed base; services are what pay for the next twenty years.

Growth drivers: data center power demand is absorbing the world's gas turbine manufacturing capacity and GE Vernova is selling it forward. In Q2-2026 it booked $24,200M of orders, +88% organically, signed 20 GW of new gas equipment contracts —18 GW of them as manufacturing slot reservations—, converted 10 GW of prior reservations into firm orders and shipped only 3 GW: gas backlog went from 44 to 53 GW and slot reservations from 56 to 63 GW, meaning 116 GW under contract, with the company targeting at least 125 GW by year-end 2026. Capacity is the bottleneck and the plan is to take it from 20 GW annualized in Q3-2026 to 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030, with industry-wide lead times stretching to eight years; in practice the factory is sold out through 2028-2029. In Electrification, data center orders exceed $5,000M year to date, more than double all of 2025, driven by the global shortage of power transformers. On that basis the company raised 2026 guidance to $45,500-$46,500M of revenue, a 12%-14% adjusted EBITDA margin and $11,500-$12,500M of free cash flow, and maintains the backlog reaches $200,000M during 2027.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Large gas turbines are a three-player oligopoly: GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power account for more than two-thirds of the machines going into plants currently under construction and over 70% of global manufacturing capacity, with GE Vernova clearly leading in Asia and the United States. There is no credible entrant: the barrier is not the patent but the foundry, the test bench and the accumulated running hours —the H-class fleet has already logged three million operating hours— and today the industry's physical bottleneck makes the scarce asset the manufacturing slot rather than the turbine itself. The judgment is a strong moat, but an uneven one and it needs to be said that way: strong in Power, where the installed base of over 7,000 turbines generates $88,463M of already-contracted services and very high switching costs; moderate in Electrification, where today's margin comes from a transformer shortage the industry is racing to fix with new capacity; and non-existent in Wind, a business that has lost money every quarter since the spin-off. The moat protects the business; it does not protect the price being paid for it.

4 Risks● Key risk: High

The half-year's cash flow is customer advances, not earningsH1-2026 operating cash flow was $10,680M, but the increase in contract liabilities (cash collected in advance) contributed $13,695M on its own, 128% of the total. Stripping it out, the business consumed −$3,015M of cash in the half. Advances went from $25,774M in December 2025 to $39,944M in June 2026
The advance is an interest-free loan repaid by delivering equipmentThe customer pays up front to reserve the manufacturing slot; GE Vernova collects today and spends that cash in 2028-2029, when it builds and delivers the turbine. As long as orders grow faster than deliveries the balance rises and cash flow looks enormous; once orders level off the mechanism reverses and cash is consumed building what has already been paid for. The guidance itself gives it away: $11,500-$12,500M of 2026 free cash flow against an implied adjusted EBITDA of $5,500-$6,400M (12%-14% on ~$46,000M). No industrial business sustainably generates twice its EBITDA in cash
Net income is inflated by a revaluation that is not moneyThe $5,398M of H1-2026 net income include a pre-tax, non-cash gain of $3,992M from remeasuring to fair value the stake the company already held in Prolec GE when it bought the remaining 50%, plus $330M from the sale of Proficy: $4,445M of gains on purchases and sales of businesses. Without them the half leaves around $1,000M. The 25.8x LTM P/E in our raw is calculated on that accounting profit and therefore means nothing: the clean multiple is the 42.0x NTM
Paying 42x earnings on a 4.2% EBIT marginAt $898.53 per share the market cap is $239,309M against LTM EBIT of $1,663M, an operating margin of 4.2% on $40,061M of revenue. The 41.95x NTM P/E is compared against a five-year median of 36.97x that in fact covers barely more than two years of trading, because the spin-off from GE dates to April 2024: the historical benchmark that makes the multiple look reasonable does not exist. The stock has traded at $1,195.94 and at $530.16 within the last 52 weeks
Wind is burning twice what was promised and offshore is still in litigationWind lost $657M of EBITDA in H1 against guidance of roughly −$400M for the full year, with orders down 40% organically in Q2. The company is retreating from offshore —it terminated its last supply agreement outside Dogger Bank and Vineyard Wind 1— but the projects in execution keep adding cost overruns and the court dispute with Vineyard Wind remains open

5 Management

LeadershipScott Strazik (CEO) and Ken Parks (CFO), the team that took the company out of General Electric in April 2024. They execute the operating side well —Power's EBITDA margin rose 320 basis points organically in the quarter— but they communicate free cash flow without separating how much comes from the business and how much is cash collected in advance
CapitalA commitment of $6,000M of capex between 2025 and 2028 (including $1,000M from Prolec GE) and $5,000M of R&D; purchase of the remaining 50% of Prolec GE, sale of Proficy and exit from China XD for roughly $600M pre-tax. In H1 it returned $3,900M to shareholders: 4.3 million shares repurchased at an average price of $854 —barely below today's $898.53— plus a $0.50 quarterly dividend. It also made a voluntary $500M contribution to the pension plan
PlanTake gas turbine output from 20 GW annualized in Q3-2026 to 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030, close the year with at least 125 GW under contract and the backlog at $200,000M during 2027, while shrinking Wind to a minimum. The plan is credible on volume; what it does not explain is what happens to cash flow in the year the advance balance stops growing
