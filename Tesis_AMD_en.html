1 Business model

Fabless designer of CPUs, GPUs and accelerators. Four segments: Data Center (EPYC and the Instinct MI300/MI400 accelerators), Client (Ryzen for PCs), Gaming (console semi-custom and Radeon) and Embedded (the Xilinx FPGA franchise). Manufacturing runs through TSMC: no fabs of its own, which is both its capital advantage and its dependency.

The engine is the data center. The MI400 (CDNA 5, TSMC 2nm, 432 GB of HBM4) targets inference, where AMD competes best. Announced anchor customers: OpenAI and Meta. NVIDIA has slipped from 90% (2024) to 86% of the data-center GPU market, and every point it cedes is worth billions. EPYC keeps taking server share from Intel.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

A genuine duopoly in x86, and an AI accelerator market with one incumbent and one challenger. NVIDIA's moat is not the silicon: it is CUDA and its millions of developers. AMD competes with ROCm — open, but with fewer mature libraries and thinner enterprise support.
