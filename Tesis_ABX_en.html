1 Business model

Vertically integrated alternative asset manager specializing in longevity assets: it buys life insurance policies in the secondary market (life settlements) directly from policyholders, originates them for institutional investors and for its own balance sheet, and earns management fees on the vehicles it runs. In 2025 revenue was $235.2M versus $111.9M in 2024, with operating income of $88.8M (versus −$0.9M) and net income attributable of $36.5M (versus a $24.0M loss). Assets under management reached ~$3.33 billion and on December 30, 2025 the stock moved from Nasdaq to the NYSE under the ticker ABX. It ended the year with 326 employees.

Growth drivers: the jump in the Asset Management segment, whose revenue went from $3.6M to $33.8M (+837%) after the December 2024 purchases of Carlisle (a Luxembourg life-settlement manager) and FCF Advisors; portfolio rotation — it sold ~68% of the policies on its balance sheet at year-end 2024 and redeployed most of that capital into new policies; the acquisition of AccuQuote (October 2025), an online life insurance brokerage; and inclusion in the Russell 2000 and 3000 indexes in September 2025, broadening the institutional base.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

The secondary market for life policies is an illiquid, lightly intermediated niche: the seller's natural counterparty is a buyer with the actuarial capability to value life expectancy, and that is where Abacus deploys its data and trading platform. It is an uncorrelated asset relative to stocks and bonds, which explains institutional demand. The flip side is that portfolio value rests on internal assumptions rather than an observable market price.
Unique asset
Proprietary longevity and life-expectancy data, plus a valuation platform (Abacus Analytics) and a tertiary trading venue
Regulatory barrier
Licensed life settlement provider in Florida and other jurisdictions; the business is regulated state by state
Vertical integration
It originates, values, trades and manages: it captures the margin across the whole chain rather than one link
Early-stage scale
~$3.33B of AUM with only 326 employees: the moat exists but is young and depends on retaining cheap access to capital
