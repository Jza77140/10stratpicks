1 Business model

Two businesses inside one company, and one funds the other. In the year ended 27-Dec-2025 net revenue was US$52,853M. Intel Products —its own chip designs— booked US$49,147M: Client Computing Group US$32,228M and Data Center and AI US$16,919M. Intel Foundry, the wafer manufacturing business (its own and third-party), booked US$17,826M, the vast majority of it internal: intersegment eliminations are US$17,683M. By geography: United States US$15,757M (29.8%), China US$12,694M (24.0%), Singapore US$9,535M (18.0%), Taiwan US$7,672M (14.5%) and the rest US$7,195M (13.6%).

The number that explains everything: Foundry loses US$10,318M at the operating line. Products earns US$12,739M (CCG +US$9,317M, DCAI +US$3,422M), but against that you subtract the foundry's US$10,318M loss and US$5,518M of unallocated corporate costs, so the whole company closed FY2025 with operating income of −US$2,214M and net income of −US$267M. Over the last twelve months the net loss widens to −US$8,382M on revenue of US$56,122M. The balance sheet shows US$211,429M of assets, US$114,281M of equity, US$44,086M of long-term debt and US$14,265M of cash; net debt is 1.2x LTM EBITDA of US$10,490M. LTM FCF is US$2,831M, a fraction of what the capacity plan costs to sustain. R&D fell to US$13,774M from US$16,546M the prior year.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

There is only one argument and it is binary: does the foundry reach breakeven or not? At US$120.00 the market capitalisation is US$634,333M and the next-twelve-months P/E is 71.3x on earnings that do not yet exist — the trailing P/E cannot be computed because the company loses money. That price is not paying for the chips Intel sells today: it is paying for a scenario in which Foundry lands volume external customers, fills the fabs and stops subtracting US$10,318M a year. What the market watches to judge that: announced external customer commitments, execution pace on the new node, and how much capital is needed before the curve turns. If the foundry works, there are two valuable companies here; if it does not, there is a mature chip company carrying a fab it cannot pay for.
