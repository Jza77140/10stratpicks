1 Business model

IonQ builds trapped-ion quantum computers and, after a wave of acquisitions, has turned into a quantum technology conglomerate that makes money four ways: selling and installing complete systems (the Forte and Tempo families) to governments, national labs and supercomputing centers; hourly access to its machines through AWS Braket, Azure Quantum and Google Cloud; multi-year research contracts with defense agencies; and, thanks to the acquisitions, quantum networking and cryptography (ID Quantique, Qubitekk), navigation and timing sensors (Vector Atomic), satellite radar (Capella Space, Skyloom) and semiconductor manufacturing (SkyWater, closed on July 31, 2026). In Q2-2026 revenue was $80.05M, up 287% year over year, with a mix of roughly 50% international, 60% commercial and 25% multi-product. Net loss for the quarter was −$1,867.7M (−$5.08 per share), but $1,650M of that figure is a NON-CASH charge from the mark-to-market revaluation of the warrants issued in October 2025: any P/E, net margin or EPS computed on that loss is broken and says nothing about the business. The number that actually describes operations is adjusted EBITDA, −$120.3M for the quarter, alongside LTM EBIT of −$1,006M and LTM free cash flow of −$483.9M. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance is $280–290M and, per the company itself, excludes any contribution from SkyWater, which on its own booked around $442M of revenue in 2025.

Growth drivers: the first vector is technical: IonQ reached 99.99% two-qubit gate fidelity, an industry record, and committed to demonstrating a 256-qubit system during 2026 using the electronic qubit control brought in by the Oxford Ionics acquisition, on the way to an architecture of more than 2,000,000 physical qubits (equivalent to 40,000–80,000 logical qubits) by 2030. The second is industrial: SkyWater contributes the only U.S.-owned, U.S.-based pure-play foundry, accredited as a Department of Defense Trusted supplier, giving IonQ in-house chip fabrication and a third-party revenue line larger than its entire current quantum business. The third is public spending: contracts with the Air Force Research Lab ($54.5M in 2024, $21.1M in January 2025 and $13.4M in July 2026), framework agreements with Sandia National Laboratories and Anduril, and the White House executive orders declaring quantum computing, sensing, networking and cybersecurity national priorities. The fourth is international turnkey system sales: the KISTI agreement for a 100-qubit machine in South Korea, the memoranda with AIST/G-QuAT in Japan and distribution through Toyota Tsusho. On top of that sit networking and security — the Tennessee quantum communications center with EPB, featuring the first commercial quantum memory installed on a live fiber network, plus the ClavisXG Multiplex product — and the space business, with 84 optical terminals in orbit and commercial InSAR capability. The contracted backlog supports the story: remaining performance obligations grew 297% year over year.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Quantum computing remains a pre-commercial market: there is still no application that beats a classical server in production, and the combined revenue of all pure plays is a rounding error inside global compute spending. IonQ competes in trapped ions against Quantinuum (backed by Honeywell), its direct rival in the same physical modality; against IBM and Google in superconducting qubits, whose balance sheets are an order of magnitude larger and who keep advancing with Heron and Willow; against PsiQuantum in photonics, QuEra and Pasqal in neutral atoms, D-Wave in quantum annealing, and Rigetti and IQM in superconductors — the latter beat IonQ to the on-premises sale at Oak Ridge. Talking about market share is premature: there is no market to split, there are research budgets. The verdict on the moat is weak, for concrete reasons: the fidelity edge is a record, not a blocking patent, and a rival physical modality can leapfrog it within one cycle; customers are mostly labs and agencies buying research rather than recurring workloads; and the cloud channel belongs to third parties — AWS, Azure and Google resell IonQ and its competitors alike — which leaves the end user with virtually no switching cost. The only genuinely defensible asset in the group is not quantum physics but regulation: the Department of Defense Trusted accreditation that comes with SkyWater, and the installed base in quantum networking and key distribution, where certifications and track record do build a barrier.
Leading gate fidelity
99.99% two-qubit gate fidelity, an industry record. A genuine technical edge, but a replicable one: it is a record, not a patent that locks anyone out.
DoD Trusted accredited foundry (SkyWater)
The only U.S.-owned, U.S.-based pure-play foundry with Department of Defense Trusted supplier accreditation. The accreditation takes years and is the hardest asset in the group to copy.
Vertical integration of the stack
QPU design, chip fabrication, integrated photonics (Nexus, Lightsynq), networking and encryption (Qubitekk, ID Quantique) and space (Capella, Skyloom) under one roof, in U.S. facilities.
Installed relationship with the U.S. government
Multi-year AFRL contracts plus agreements with Sandia and Anduril. Security clearances and an execution track record are an entry barrier that capital alone cannot buy.
Distribution across all three clouds
Available on AWS Braket, Azure Quantum and Google Cloud, giving global reach without an owned sales force. The flip side: those same clouds also offer the competitors.

4 Risks● Key risk: Critical

Valuation: about 62x salesA $15,306M market cap on roughly $246M of LTM revenue. To support that on a mature 8x sales multiple, revenue would have to reach about $1,900M at a positive margin: nearly 6.7 times the 2026 guidance of $285M. At 40% compound annual growth that is about six years without a single stumble; at 25% it is more than eight. That is the assumption holding up the price.
Structural shareholder dilutionAverage shares went from 250.97M in Q2-2025 to 367.66M in Q2-2026, up 46.5% in one year. On top of that, 43.01M seven-year warrants with a $155 exercise price remain outstanding, plus the roughly 24M shares handed over for SkyWater. The $3,000M cash pile was not generated by the business: it was generated by issuing stock.
Operating cash burnAdjusted EBITDA for the quarter was −$120.3M and LTM free cash flow −$483.9M. With roughly $2,000M of pro forma cash after paying for SkyWater, the runway is measured in years, and only if the acquisition pace slows; if it does not, the issuance comes back.
Eight acquisitions in 20 months, none integratedQubitekk, ID Quantique, Lightsynq, Oxford Ionics, Capella Space, Vector Atomic, Skyloom, Nexus Photonics and SkyWater blend ion physics, satellites, inertial sensors and a semiconductor foundry. The reported 287% mixes purchases with operations, and the company's own '100% organic growth' is a self-defined metric that excludes, at its discretion, everything acquired after 2024.
Commercial use still unproven at scaleThere is currently no quantum application that beats classical computing in production. The typical buyer is still a lab or an agency buying research. If useful quantum advantage slips five years, system revenue depends on public budgets rather than economic demand.

5 Management

LeadershipNiccolo de Masi, CEO since February 2025 and Chairman since August 2025, when Peter Chapman left the board. His background is Glu Mobile, Resideo and a SPAC, not physics; Inder Singh is COO and CFO, formerly CFO of Arm. The team's profile is capital markets and M&A, which is exactly what the company executed over the past 20 months.
CapitalRaised close to $2,950M in equity during 2025 (14.17M shares at $55.49 in July and 16.5M at $93.00 in October, the latter bundled with $155 warrants) and paid for SkyWater with $741M in cash plus roughly 24M shares, on a total deal value of about $1,800M. The stock is the currency it buys with: the cost of growth is paid in dilution, not in interest.
PlanDemonstrate 256 qubits in 2026 using Oxford Ionics' electronic qubit control and scale to more than 2,000,000 physical qubits (40,000–80,000 logical) by 2030, funding the path with SkyWater foundry revenue and defense spending. The fragile assumption is one of timing: that logical qubits arrive before the market's willingness to fund the journey through share issuance runs out.
