1 Business model

The world's largest fast-food chain, with ~95% of its more than 43,000 restaurants franchised. On LTM sales of $27,702M it generates EBIT of $12,805M (~46% margin) and FCF of $7,761M (~28% margin), built on a royalty-and-rent model that shifts almost all day-to-day operating risk to the franchisee. It trades at a 21.2x LTM P/E.

Growth drivers: recovering U.S. traffic after the -4.5% drop in Q2'26 — which management itself attributed to its own execution mistakes rather than structural weakness: a value menu designed with enough pricing flexibility that franchisees raised rather than lowered prices, compounded by pulling a digital deal that kept frequent customers loyal — plus roughly 2,600 new restaurants opening globally in 2026 and acceleration in International Operated Markets.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

McDonald's competes in the value segment of fast food against Burger King, Wendy's, Chipotle and local chains in each market, against a backdrop of a pressured lower-income consumer. It defends the category's best-known value positioning, though it lost relative traffic in 2026 due to its own pricing decisions, which it is now correcting.
Real estate scale
Owns or leases much of the land where franchisees operate, with rents tied to sales
Global brand
Brand recognition and local presence that are hard to match across more than 100 countries
Franchise economics
~90% of total operating margin comes from franchising, with minimal capital committed; only ~10% of restaurants are company-operated
Purchasing power
Sourcing scale that sustains the value menu without destroying franchisee margin
