1 Business model

Oklo does not sell reactors: it builds, owns and operates them, selling the electricity under long-term power purchase agreements. The product is the Aurora, a below-grade sodium-cooled fast reactor running on metallic HALEU fuel, a direct descendant of the EBR-II that operated on the same Idaho site from 1964 to 1994; it is currently sized at 50-75 MWe. There are three lines: Power (Aurora), Fuel (the A3F plant at INL plus a recycling project in Tennessee) and Isotopes, which arrived with the acquisition of Atomic Alchemy and whose Groves test reactor in Texas was built in 229 days and reached first criticality in 2026. That is the source of the only revenue in the company's entire history: $1.21 million in Q2 2026. Against that, the LTM through 30-Jun-2026 shows an operating loss of -$217.57 million, a net loss of -$152.77 million and free cash flow of -$275.82 million. Publishing a P/E or a sales multiple on this company would be a lie: the $7,671 million valuation rests on regulatory and construction milestones, not on results, and the balance sheet — roughly $3,000 million in cash and investments — is today the main asset, not the business.

Focos de crecimiento: the declared pipeline is around 14 GW, but it is worth looking at what it is made of. The Master Power Agreement with Switch for up to 12 GW through 2044 and the 500 MW letter of intent with Equinix over 20 years are the two large tranches; the only one with confirmed customer money is Equinix, with a $25 million prepayment. Then come a 50 MW LOI with Diamondback, a 1.2 GW campus in Pike County, Ohio backed by Meta, and a notice of intent to award at Eielson Air Force Base that is not yet a contract. The second leg is fuel: the A3F plant at Idaho National Laboratory obtained its Department of Energy nuclear safety design agreement in November 2025. The third is medical and industrial isotopes, which is the line that actually bills. The timetable the company maintains is first power from Aurora at INL around 2028.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

The advanced modular reactor field is crowded and nobody is selling power yet. NuScale is the only one with a complete NRC design certification (light water, 77 MWe per module); X-energy works with high-temperature gas and is going public in 2026; TerraPower is developing Natrium, also sodium-cooled but 345 MWe and paired with molten-salt storage; Kairos uses fluoride salts with TRISO fuel and was the first non-light-water design approved for construction by the NRC; BWXT and Nano Nuclear compete in microreactors. Oklo's technical difference is the fast spectrum with sodium and metallic HALEU, which would let it burn recycled fuel; the commercial difference is that it keeps the asset, promising recurring revenue in exchange for far more of its own capital. The moat is weak, and that should be said plainly: there is no operating licence, not a single firm power purchase agreement, the sodium technology is not proprietary — it is the design the United States government itself ran for thirty years — and the barriers that do exist, regulatory and capital, protect every competitor equally. What Oklo has today is not a moat but a position advantage: it was the first to have an advanced-fission combined licence application accepted by the NRC and the only company in the DOE Reactor Pilot Program selected for two projects.
Regulatory head start
First advanced-fission combined licence application accepted for review by the NRC, and the only DOE Reactor Pilot Program participant with two approved projects. This is a calendar advantage, not a barrier: any competitor can walk the same path.
Site access and the INL legacy
Aurora is being built at Idaho National Laboratory on the technical inheritance of the EBR-II, which ran there from 1964 to 1994. Access to that site and to the reactor's operating data is not available to just anyone.
Vertical integration of fuel
The A3F plant plus the Tennessee recycling project aim to produce Oklo's own HALEU. If it works, it solves the bottleneck currently holding back the whole industry; if it does not, Oklo depends on the same scarce supply as everyone else.
Build-own-operate model
By keeping the asset and selling power under PPAs it captures the full margin instead of a manufacturer's margin. The cost is a capital requirement on the order of $14,000 million through the mid-2040s.
Isotope reactor already critical
Groves reached first criticality in 2026 on private land, a precedent no other company holds, and it is the only part of the business generating revenue today.

4 Risks● Key risk: Critical

The Meta-backed anchor project was pulled from the interconnection queuePJM removed the Pike County campus on 03-Aug-2026 over application deficiencies, including failure to demonstrate voltage ride-through capability. Oklo filed an emergency complaint with FERC on 28-Aug-2026 and concedes a delay of at least 14 months.
There is no NRC licenceThe first application was denied without prejudice on 06-Jan-2022 and the new one is only now under review. The 2028 start-up hangs on the Department of Energy route, which is an administrative and reversible authorisation, not a commercial licence.
The 14 GW are letters of intent that can be withdrawnNeither Switch nor Meta nor Eielson is contractually obliged to buy. The only confirmed customer money is the $25 million Equinix prepayment. Reading the pipeline as backlog is a category error.
Structural capital needs and dilutionShares went from 137.7 million at the end of 2024 to 186.0 million in August 2026: +35% in 19 months. The 2026 at-the-market tranches were placed at average net prices of $96.95 and $63.51; at $41.24 the same cash costs between 1.5 and 2.4 times more shares.
