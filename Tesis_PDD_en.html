1 Business model

PDD Holdings operates Pinduoduo, China's largest group-buying and discount marketplace, and Temu, its cross-border, direct-to-consumer platform selling in more than 50 countries. Revenue splits roughly evenly between online marketing services (merchant advertising) and transaction services (sales commissions). FCF is high relative to sales, but pressured by the subsidies the company funnels into Temu to gain international scale.

In China, PDD launched "high-quality" merchant support programs to curb the price war that had been eroding margins.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

The moat comes from the network effect of a two-sided marketplace — buyers and merchants — reinforced by the group-buying model, but it is fiercely contested: Alibaba, JD.com and Douyin compete closely in China, while Amazon and Shein pressure Temu abroad. The corporate structure uses a variable interest entity (VIE) to operate under restricted Chinese licenses; in 2025 that VIE accounted for just 13% of total revenue, with the rest generated outside that structure (mainly Temu), which partly — not fully — reduces exposure to Chinese regulation.
