1 Business model

Canadian-based intermediate gold producer with three operations in North America: the Island Gold District (Island Gold and Magino mines) and Young-Davidson in Northern Ontario, and the Mulatos District (Mulatos and La Yaqui Grande) in Sonora, Mexico. It reports under IFRS in U.S. dollars and files a Form 40-F with the SEC, not a 10-K. In 2025 it produced 545,400 ounces of gold (-4% year over year, below revised guidance) and sold 531,230 ounces at an average realized price of $3,372 per ounce, generating record revenues of $1,808.8M (+34%). Net earnings were $885.8M ($2.11 per share) and adjusted net earnings $587.1M ($1.40), with record free cash flow of $351.7M. It employs more than 2,400 people and closed the year with Mineral Reserves of 15.9 million ounces (+32%).

Growth drivers: the Phase 3+ Shaft Expansion at the Island Gold District, with the shaft sunk to 1,350 metres (98% of final depth) and completion expected in the fourth quarter of 2026; the IGD Expansion to 20,000 tonnes per day, targeting average annual production of 534,000 ounces from 2028 at AISC of $1,025 per ounce; the Lynn Lake project in Manitoba, averaging 186,000 ounces per year over its first 10 years at AISC of $829 per ounce with first production expected in 2029; and the PDA project in the Mulatos District, under construction with initial production expected by mid-2027. Three-year guidance points to 570,000-650,000 ounces in 2026 (+12%) and 755,000-835,000 ounces in 2028 (+46%).

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Gold is a commodity and the company is a price taker: it does not compete on product but on cost curve position, deposit quality and jurisdiction. Alamos operates in two established mining jurisdictions — Canada and Mexico — and its structural advantage lies in the grade of the Island Gold underground deposit (5.1 million ounces at 10.61 g/t) and a 19-year mine life in that district. The scale of its reserves (15.9 Moz) and a net cash balance sheet allow it to internally fund its entire growth plan.
Unique asset
The Island Gold underground hosts 5.1 million ounces at a grade of 10.61 g/t, a high-grade deposit that cannot be replicated
Low costs
The expansion study projects AISC of $1,025 per ounce at the Island Gold District from 2028, roughly 31% below the $1,524 recorded in 2025
Regulatory barrier
Permits and environmental impact assessments already secured, including the Mexican amendment of Jan-2025 that enabled construction of PDA
No product moat
Gold sells at the market price: there is no pricing power and no customer loyalty
