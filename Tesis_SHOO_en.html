1 Business model

Designs, sources and markets fashion footwear, accessories and apparel under owned and licensed brands (Steve Madden, Kurt Geiger, Carvela, Dolce Vita, Betsey Johnson, Blondo, ATM, Anne Klein) plus a private label business. It sells wholesale to more than 2,000 retailers and direct-to-consumer through 399 stores and seven e-commerce platforms. Revenue reached $2,534.1M (+11.0%) in 2025, driven by the May-2025 acquisition of Kurt Geiger, which contributed $402.8M of revenue and a $50.6M net loss in the post-acquisition period. Profitability collapsed: net income fell to $44.7M ($0.63 per share) from $169.4M ($2.35) in 2024, with operating income of $80.8M versus $224.9M.

Growth drivers: integrating and scaling Kurt Geiger and Carvela globally, which took international sales to $856.0M (more than double the $424.2M of 2024); expanding the direct-to-consumer channel, already 33.4% of revenue; diversifying sourcing away from China, which fell from 76.6% of purchases in 2024 to 56.1% in 2025 while Cambodia rose from 13.8% to 24.6%; and the 'test-and-react' model that uses owned stores as a trend laboratory.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

Fashion is a hypercompetitive market with low barriers to entry and short trend cycles. Steven Madden's advantage is speed to market and design curation rather than technology or scale. The supply chain is outsourced across Asia, which exposes margin directly to tariffs and U.S. trade policy.
Brand
Steve Madden and Kurt Geiger have standalone recognition and consumer permission to extend across categories
Speed to market
Designing, testing in owned stores and reacting quickly lets it catch trends with less dead inventory
Limited moat
There are no switching costs or recurring revenue: in fashion the advantage must be re-earned every season
