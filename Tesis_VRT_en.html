1 Business model

Designs, manufactures, installs and services the critical digital infrastructure of data centers: AC and DC power management, low and medium voltage switchgear, busbar, racks, single-phase UPS, energy storage and air- and liquid-cooled thermal management for AI accelerated compute. It operates engineering, manufacturing and service sites in more than 40 countries and employs roughly 34,000 people; its brands include Vertiv, Liebert, NetSure, Geist, Energy Labs, ERS, Albér and Avocent. In 2025 net sales reached $10,229.9M (+27.7%) versus $8,011.8M in 2024, with operating profit of $1,829.7M. The combined order backlog reached $15.0 billion at 31-Dec-2025, against $7.2 billion a year earlier; most of it is considered firm and is expected to ship within 12 to 18 months.

3 Competitive advantage (MOAT)

The critical infrastructure market is concentrated among a few global players — Schneider Electric, Eaton, ABB — plus niche competitors such as Delta Electronics, Stulz, Johnson Controls and Socomec. The business holds up because power and cooling equipment is mission critical: a failure stops the data center, so the decision is driven by reliability and service capability before price. The global lifecycle service network on the installed base generates recurring revenue ($2,022.9M of services and spares in 2025).
